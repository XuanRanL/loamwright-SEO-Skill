# Voice: Playful

Humor-driven without being annoying. Smart wit + cultural references + a sense of being in on the joke. Reader is treated as a fellow traveler who enjoys the same things you do. Not just casual — actively trying to be FUN.

## Best for

- Gaming content
- Pop culture content
- Memes / internet culture
- Travel content (with humor)
- Food + drink (lighter side)
- Tech humor / "dev jokes"
- Lifestyle B2C
- Content for younger demographics
- Newsletter / personal brand
- Comedy-adjacent content

## Hard rules

1. **Smart humor, not dad jokes** — wit > puns; observation > setup-punchline
2. **Cultural awareness** — references that respect your audience's intelligence
3. **Earned humor** — set it up; deliver; move on
4. **No forced humor** — silence is OK; not every paragraph needs a joke
5. **Self-aware** — acknowledge the meta when relevant
6. **Punch up, not down** — humor at expense of yourself or absurdity, not vulnerable groups
7. **Tonal shift is OK** — be funny then serious then funny again

## Voice characteristics

| Trait | Setting |
|---|---|
| Contractions | Always |
| First person | Common (I, we) |
| Direct address | Frequent (you, we) |
| Humor | Encouraged but not in every sentence |
| Sentence length | Highly variable (3 words to 35+) |
| Burstiness | High (SD > 8) |
| Paragraph length | Variable; comedic timing matters |
| Tone | Smart, mischievous, occasionally tender |

## Vocabulary preferences

| Prefer | Avoid |
|---|---|
| "It's giving..." (when appropriate) | "It is reminiscent of..." (formal) |
| "no notes." | "It is well-executed" |
| "Anyway." (paragraph break) | "However," |
| "Look —" | "It must be considered that..." |
| "And then it just... worked." | "The result was favorable." |
| "Friend." (when calling out reader) | "Dear reader," |

## Required moves

### Smart references

Pop culture, memes, internet humor — but tied to your point. Lazy references break trust.

✗ "It's giving 'normal article'." (forced — not why we're here)
✓ "If you've ever spent 20 minutes choosing a Netflix show, you'll recognize this feeling: too many options. Same energy with marketing tools." (earned)

### Setup + payoff

Build a small joke arc:
- Setup the expectation
- Subvert it
- Move on (don't milk)

### Use silence too

Not every paragraph needs humor. Sometimes the comedy comes from contrast — restrained next to playful.

### Acknowledge the absurd

When something is genuinely funny in your topic, name it:

> "Here's where it gets weird. Most CRM software costs more than my first car. And they all want me to schedule a demo."

### Self-deprecating done right

Make fun of yourself, not your reader.

✓ "I spent two years thinking 'on premises' meant 'in the office.' It does not."
✗ "Anyone who doesn't know this is a fool."

## Banned in playful voice

- Mean humor at vulnerable groups
- Forced puns ("It's quite the [topic]-er!")
- Twitter-clone humor without your own voice
- Trying-too-hard ("frfr no cap fr")
- Outdated memes (always check the meme is alive)
- Reference flooding (more than 1 per paragraph)
- "lol" in body text (use in headlines, captions OK)
- Excessive emojis (1-3 per piece max)
- Sarcasm without clear signal (reader can't tell intent)

## Sample passage

### Bad (playful done wrong — trying too hard)
> OMG you guys, marketing tools are SO confusing!!! 😂😂😂 Like, why are there a MILLION of them? It's giving... chaos? It's giving... math homework? frfr the marketing software industry is unhinged 💀💀💀 No notes besides EVERY NOTE.

### Good (playful voice + general purpose)
> There are approximately 12,000 marketing software products on the market. I checked. I did not enjoy checking.
>
> Twelve thousand. Most of them have logos that look like they were generated by the same AI image generator, with names like "Convertly" and "Optomize" and "Leadify-Pro" and at least four called variations of "Hubsville."
>
> The good news: most of them are bad. The bad news: figuring out which is which requires either trying them all yourself (which: no) or trusting a review site (which: maybe).
>
> What follows is a guide to actually choosing one. I have used roughly 30 of these tools across the past decade. I have opinions. Some of them are unpopular.

## Combining with purposes

| Voice + purpose | Typical use |
|---|---|
| `playful + general` | Pop culture + tech humor blogs |
| `playful + email` | Newsletter / personal brand |
| `playful + essay` | Substack-style humor essays |
| `playful + marketing` | DTC / lifestyle ecom (use carefully) |
| `playful + technical` | Engineering humor (dev humor) |

## When NOT to use

- Legal / compliance content
- Medical / health YMYL
- Financial advice
- Crisis communication
- B2B enterprise sales
- News reporting
- Documentation (use teacher)
- Bereavement / sensitive topics

## Industry warnings

These industries should NOT use playful voice:
- Finance (use authoritative or professional)
- Legal (use authoritative)
- Health / medical (use professional or warm)
- Insurance (use professional or authoritative)
- Death care / funeral (use warm)
- Crisis support (use warm)

## Soul Injection compatibility

Heavy compatibility — playful is built for soul:

- ✓ #1 Strong opinions (often comedic ones)
- ✓ #2 Honest uncertainty (with self-deprecation)
- ✓ #3 Specific sensory detail (concrete > abstract for humor)
- ✓ #4 Shared experience callback ("you know that feeling")
- ✓ #5 Allow tangents (controlled — for comedy)
- ✓ #6 Dramatic paragraph variation (timing matters)
- ✓ #7 Imperfect opening
- ✓ #8 Break parallel structure (surprise)
- ✓ #9 Callbacks (running gags)
- ✓ #10 Self-correct ("wait — that's wrong")
- ✓ #11 End without wrap-up (anti-climactic = funny)

ALL 11 are encouraged. This is the most soul-rich voice.
