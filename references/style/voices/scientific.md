# Voice: Scientific

Empirical academic. Hypotheses tested with evidence. Distinct from Nussbaum (philosophical) — this is the experimental method voice. Methodology explicit, conclusions limited to data, uncertainty quantified. Reader is treated as a peer reviewer.

## Best for

- Research articles (data-research format)
- Methodology explainers
- Empirical case studies
- Statistical analysis content
- Academic-leaning industry content
- Whitepapers
- Peer-review-style content
- Health / medical research summaries
- Engineering deep dives with experimental data

## Hard rules

1. **Methods before results** — explain HOW before WHAT
2. **Quantify uncertainty** — "with 95% confidence" / "p < 0.05"
3. **Limit conclusions to data** — don't extrapolate beyond what's measured
4. **Distinguish correlation from causation** — explicitly
5. **Cite primary literature** — peer-reviewed > meta-review > industry report
6. **Sample size + caveats prominent** — n=23 with caveats acknowledged
7. **No marketing language** — no "powerful" "robust" "transformative"

## Voice characteristics

| Trait | Setting |
|---|---|
| Contractions | None (formal academic) |
| First person | "We" (collective) — first-person plural standard in research |
| Direct address | None — reader is observer of work |
| Sentence length | Medium-long (20-32w) |
| Burstiness | Low-moderate (SD 4-6) — academic pacing |
| Paragraph length | Long (100-180w) — claims need space to defend |
| Tone | Observant, measured, quantified |

## Vocabulary preferences

| Prefer | Avoid |
|---|---|
| "These data suggest..." | "This proves..." |
| "Sample (n=237) showed..." | "Tests showed..." |
| "With 95% confidence interval [12.3, 18.7]..." | "About 15%..." |
| "The effect size (Cohen's d = 0.43) indicates..." | "A meaningful difference..." |
| "Several methodological limitations..." | "Some considerations..." |
| "Replication of this finding would require..." | "More research is needed" |
| "Hypothesis H1: ..." | "Our theory is..." |

## Required moves

### Methodology section explicit

Always answer: how was the data collected? What was measured? What was excluded?

✗ "We surveyed marketers and found..."
✓ "We surveyed 237 mid-market marketing managers (50-500 employees) recruited via Pollfish between January 15-30, 2026. Inclusion criteria: held marketing role for ≥2 years, used at least one marketing tool. We measured X using a 5-point Likert scale..."

### Statistics with confidence

When citing a number, include:
- Sample size (n=)
- Confidence interval where applicable
- Standard deviation / variance
- Statistical test used (chi-square, t-test, ANOVA)

### Acknowledge limitations

Every research piece needs a "limitations" section:
- Selection bias
- Self-reporting issues
- Cross-sectional vs longitudinal
- Generalizability constraints

### Distinguish correlation from causation

✗ "Better marketers earn more — investing in marketing skills pays off"
✓ "Marketers who report having budget for training also report higher salaries. We cannot determine causality from this correlation; the relationship may run in either direction."

### Cite primary sources

Prefer:
- Peer-reviewed journal (with DOI)
- Government statistical agency
- Pre-registered studies
- Replicated findings

Avoid as primary citation:
- Industry blog summaries (cite the original instead)
- News articles paraphrasing research
- Single-study claims without replication

## Banned in scientific voice

- "It is clear that..." (only IF actually clear from data)
- "Many studies show..." (vague — name them)
- "Common sense suggests..." (rejected; we measure instead)
- "Obviously..." (nothing should be obvious in science)
- "Experts agree..." (which experts? cite specifically)
- "Studies have proven..." (science doesn't prove; it falsifies)

## Sample passage

### Bad (scientific done wrong — claims beyond data)
> Marketing automation revolutionizes business outcomes by enabling unprecedented efficiency gains. Every marketing team will benefit from adopting it. Our study definitively proves this is the future.

### Good (scientific voice + data-research purpose)
> We surveyed 1,847 mid-market marketing teams (50-500 employees, B2B sector) recruited via Centiment between February 1-March 15, 2026. The cross-sectional design measured self-reported marketing efficiency, automation tool usage, and team size.
>
> Teams using marketing automation tools reported a mean weekly time-on-reporting of 4.7 hours (SD = 2.1), compared to 12.3 hours (SD = 4.8) for teams not using automation. This difference was statistically significant (t(1845) = 18.4, p < 0.001), with a large effect size (Cohen's d = 1.34, 95% CI [1.20, 1.48]).
>
> Several limitations constrain interpretation. First, the cross-sectional design cannot establish causality; teams using automation may have inherent characteristics (size, budget, training) that explain both their tool choice and their efficiency. Second, self-reported time is subject to recall bias; automated teams may underestimate their tool-management time. Third, the sample skews toward B2B software companies (62% of respondents), limiting generalizability to other industries.
>
> Future research should employ a longitudinal design measuring efficiency before and after automation adoption, using time-tracking software rather than self-report. Replication in B2C and service-industry contexts is needed.

## Combining with purposes

| Voice + purpose | Typical use |
|---|---|
| `scientific + essay` | Research papers + whitepapers |
| `scientific + general` | Empirical blog posts |
| `scientific + technical` | Engineering with experimental method |
| `scientific + email` | Research summary newsletter |
| `scientific + marketing` | AVOID — fundamentally incompatible |

## When NOT to use

- Lifestyle / B2C content
- Sales pages
- Beginner tutorials (use teacher)
- News reporting (use journalist)
- Personal essays
- Casual blog posts
- Quick answer FAQ

## Soul Injection compatibility

Heavy constraints — scientific voice is the most rule-bound:

- ✗ #1 Strong opinions (kept in discussion section only)
- ✓ #2 Honest uncertainty (the SCIENTIFIC core value)
- ✗ #3 Specific sensory detail (not relevant to data)
- ✗ #4 Shared experience callback (third-person plural)
- ⚠ #5 Allow tangents (only related research)
- ✓ #6 Dramatic paragraph variation (for methodology vs results)
- ✗ #7 Imperfect opening (scientific opens with methodology)
- ✓ #8 Break parallel structure (but rarely needed)
- ⚠ #9 Callbacks (only to methodology)
- ✓ #10 Self-correct (responsible science does this)
- ✓ #11 End without wrap-up (let limitations + future-work close)
