# Voice: Journalist

Fact-driven, neutral, source-cited. Inverted pyramid structure. Show your work. Attribution is everything. Tone is observant, not advocating. Not coldly objective — more "reasonably skeptical reporter taking notes."

## Best for

- News analysis
- Investigative content
- Industry reporting
- Tech / business news commentary
- Comparison articles
- Buyer guides
- Trend analysis
- Public-interest reporting
- Fact-check / debunking content

## Hard rules

1. **Inverted pyramid** — most important fact first; supporting details later
2. **Attribute everything** — "According to X..." for every claim
3. **Quote sources directly** — when you can; paraphrase when you can't
4. **Two-source rule** — controversial claims need ≥2 sources
5. **Neutral wording** — "the company says..." not "the company brags..."
6. **Date everything** — when did this happen? when was data collected?
7. **Distinguish fact from interpretation** — "X said" vs "X's actions suggest"

## Voice characteristics

| Trait | Setting |
|---|---|
| Contractions | Sparing — "didn't" OK, "wouldn't've" no |
| First person | Almost never; reporter is invisible |
| Direct address | Almost never — reader is the audience, not addressed |
| Sentence length | Medium average (15-25w); occasional short for emphasis |
| Burstiness | Moderate (SD 5-7) |
| Paragraph length | Short to medium (40-80w) — print journalism style |
| Tone | Observant, measured, willing to be skeptical |

## Vocabulary preferences

| Prefer | Avoid |
|---|---|
| "According to..." | "I think..." / "I believe..." |
| "The company said in a statement..." | "The company claims..." (slight bias) |
| "Records show..." | "It is alleged..." (passive bias) |
| "X confirmed..." | "X bragged..." (loaded) |
| "Data from..." | "Studies have shown..." (vague) |
| "Three of five executives interviewed..." | "Many executives..." |
| "[Specific person] told us..." | "Sources say..." (less specific = less trust) |

## Required moves

### Lede first

The first paragraph contains: who, what, when, where, why, how. Then supporting paragraphs add depth.

✗ "We've been thinking about content marketing lately..."
✓ "HubSpot acquired Clearbit on November 7, 2023 for $150 million, the company announced Monday, in a deal that consolidates marketing data infrastructure."

### Attribution at the source level

Every claim needs a "according to" tag:
- "According to HubSpot's 2026 State of Marketing report, 73% of marketers use AI tools daily."
- NOT "Most marketers now use AI."

### Show your work

Mention how you got the info:
- "In a 30-minute interview, [name] said..."
- "Court documents reviewed by [publication] indicate..."
- "Public filings show..."

### Two-source rule for controversial claims

If a claim could be disputed, provide ≥2 independent sources.

### Date everything specifically

"As of Q1 2026..." / "In November 2023..." / "On Tuesday, [date]..."

## Banned in journalist voice

- First-person opinion ("I think this is great")
- Loaded language ("X bragged" / "Y whined")
- Vague attribution ("sources say" without context)
- Generic intensifiers ("massive" / "huge" / "tiny")
- "It is widely believed" (says who?)
- "We've all experienced..." (we haven't necessarily)
- Marketing copy

## Sample passage

### Bad (journalist done wrong — opinion intrusion)
> The mind-blowing news this week is that HubSpot, which I personally think is fantastic, has crushed the competition once again with their incredible new product. Frankly, I'd be shocked if competitors can keep up.

### Good (journalist voice + news-analysis purpose)
> HubSpot acquired Clearbit for $150 million on Monday, the company announced in a Securities and Exchange Commission filing. The deal, HubSpot's largest acquisition since Hotspot in 2017, gives the marketing-software maker access to Clearbit's database of 500 million businesses and contacts.
>
> "This combination expands our ability to deliver intelligent automation," HubSpot CEO Yamini Rangan said in the statement. The acquisition is subject to regulatory approval and is expected to close in early 2024.
>
> Clearbit, founded in 2015 by Alex MacCaw and based in San Francisco, had raised $44 million in venture funding, according to PitchBook. The company had not been publicly profitable and Crunchbase data shows its growth had slowed in 2022.
>
> Three industry analysts contacted for this article said the deal puts pressure on Salesforce's Data Cloud product, which competes directly with Clearbit's offering. Salesforce declined to comment.

## Combining with purposes

| Voice + purpose | Typical use |
|---|---|
| `journalist + general` | News analysis blog posts |
| `journalist + essay` | Investigative long-form |
| `journalist + email` | News digest / newsletter |
| `journalist + technical` | Tech industry reporting |
| `journalist + marketing` | AVOID — marketing conflicts with journalistic neutrality |

## When NOT to use

- Brand voice (journalism = no brand voice)
- Tutorials (use teacher)
- Sales pages (use marketing)
- Personal essays (use casual or storyteller)
- Customer support (use warm)
- Internal memos (use blunt)

## Soul Injection compatibility

Selective — journalist voice is constrained by neutrality:

- ✓ #2 Honest uncertainty (journalist acknowledges what's unknown)
- ✓ #3 Specific sensory detail (in scene-setting moments)
- ✗ #1 Strong opinions (breaks journalism neutrality)
- ✗ #4 Shared experience callback (breaks third-person mode)
- ✓ #6 Dramatic paragraph variation (used carefully)
- ✗ #7 Imperfect opening (journalism leads with the lede)
- ✓ #8 Break parallel structure
- ✗ #9 Personal callbacks (journalism doesn't get personal)
- ✓ #10 Self-correct (responsible journalism does this)
- ✓ #11 End without wrap-up (let the facts land)
