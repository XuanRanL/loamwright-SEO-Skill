# Voice: Authoritative

Confident expert voice. Definitive answers backed by credentials + data. Reader is treated as someone seeking expertise, not a friend or learner. Tone respects the reader's intelligence while owning the knowledge gap.

## Best for

- Finance / fintech / investment content
- Legal content
- Real estate (commercial + residential)
- Insurance content
- Tax / accounting content
- High-end consulting
- Medical advice from credentialed practitioners (with YMYL caveat)
- Compliance / regulatory content
- Expert reviews (with deep domain knowledge)

## Hard rules

1. **Definitive language** — "X is the case" not "X might be the case"
2. **Credentials forward** — author bio + experience visible
3. **Specific recommendations** — pick one, name reasons
4. **Defend the position** — engage opposing views, refute with evidence
5. **Cite expertise sources** — peer-reviewed, official, recognized authorities
6. **Address the asymmetry** — reader doesn't know; author does
7. **No false modesty** — confident statements when warranted

## Voice characteristics

| Trait | Setting |
|---|---|
| Contractions | Sparing — formal-leaning |
| First person | Used when sharing professional judgment |
| Direct address | Selective — used to give recommendations |
| Sentence length | Medium-long (18-28w typical) |
| Burstiness | Moderate (SD 5-7) |
| Paragraph length | Medium (60-100w) — let claims have space |
| Tone | Confident, considered, willing to be specific |

## Vocabulary preferences

| Prefer | Avoid |
|---|---|
| "The data shows..." | "It might be that..." |
| "Our recommendation is..." | "You could consider..." |
| "In our 20-year practice..." | "Many experts suggest..." (vague) |
| "Three factors determine X:" | "Various factors influence X" |
| "This approach reduces risk by N%" | "This approach helps with risk" |
| "Most professionals avoid X for one reason:" | "There are concerns about X" |
| "Specifically: ..." | "Generally speaking..." |

## Required moves

### Open with definitive statement

✗ "There are many factors to consider when investing..."
✓ "Most retail investors lose money on options trading. The data on this is clear: 70-80% of retail options traders lose money over a 1-year period."

### Author credentials visible

Inline reference to credentials:
- "In our practice managing $400M in client assets..."
- "After 15 years at the FDA, I can say..."
- "Of the 200+ M&A deals I've reviewed..."

### Specific recommendations

When asked "what should I do?" — answer specifically.

✗ "It depends on your situation. Talk to a professional."
✓ "If your portfolio is over 60% equities and you're within 10 years of retirement, you should rebalance. The specific allocation that historically minimizes max drawdown is 60/40 stocks/bonds with a 15% allocation to TIPS."

### Defend with evidence

When making a claim, defend it:
- "X is true because of A, B, and C."
- Cite the underlying data, study, regulation

### Address opposing views

Steel-man the opposing position. Then explain why your position holds.

## Banned in authoritative voice

- "Everyone has a different view" (no specific recommendation)
- "It really depends" (cop-out without specifying what it depends on)
- "Many factors are at play" (lazy)
- "You should consult a professional" (without offering specific guidance first)
- "There are no easy answers" (true but unhelpful)
- Sycophantic openers ("Great question!")
- False modesty ("I'm not sure but...")

## Sample passage

### Bad (authoritative done wrong — arrogant)
> Look, I've been doing this for 20 years and you obviously haven't. The answer is X. Period. If you can't see why, you're not thinking clearly. Anyone with experience knows this.

### Good (authoritative voice + general purpose)
> A 60/40 stocks/bonds allocation is no longer the standard "balanced portfolio" benchmark. The data over the past two market cycles suggests adding 10-15% allocation to short-duration Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities (TIPS).
>
> Here's why. Between 2020 and 2024, traditional 60/40 portfolios experienced max drawdown of -16.9%, while 60/30/10 portfolios with TIPS allocation showed max drawdown of -12.4% — a meaningful 4.5 percentage-point reduction in worst-case losses.
>
> The arithmetic matters most when withdrawing in retirement. A retiree pulling 4% annually from a portfolio with -16.9% peak drawdown faces materially worse sequence-of-returns risk than the same retiree pulling 4% from a -12.4% drawdown portfolio.
>
> Some practitioners disagree, arguing that TIPS underperform during periods of falling inflation. That objection is valid in isolation but misses the diversification benefit: TIPS' real return is negatively correlated with the equity risk premium during inflation regime changes. This is precisely when you want them.
>
> The recommendation: 60% diversified equities, 30% intermediate-duration Treasuries, 10% TIPS. Rebalance annually. This is not optimization; it is risk reduction with maintained return potential.

## Combining with purposes

| Voice + purpose | Typical use |
|---|---|
| `authoritative + essay` | Long-form thought leadership |
| `authoritative + general` | Standard expert blog post |
| `authoritative + email` | Professional advisory newsletter |
| `authoritative + technical` | Technical white papers |
| `authoritative + marketing` | High-end professional services |

## When NOT to use

- B2C lifestyle (use casual or storyteller)
- Educational content for beginners (use teacher)
- Personal stories (use storyteller)
- News reporting (use journalist)
- Casual blog posts (use casual)

## YMYL caveat

For Your Money or Your Life topics (health, finance, legal):
- Authoritative voice REQUIRES documented credentials
- Without credentials, voice should be `professional + general`
- T05 veto fires if YMYL content uses authoritative voice without author credentials

## Soul Injection compatibility

Selective — authoritative voice is constrained by confidence requirements:

- ✓ #1 Strong opinions (the signature move)
- ⚠ #2 Honest uncertainty (use only on genuinely uncertain claims)
- ✓ #3 Specific sensory detail (in case-example moments)
- ⚠ #4 Shared experience callback (only when professional shared)
- ✓ #6 Dramatic paragraph variation (for emphasis)
- ⚠ #7 Imperfect opening (authoritative usually opens with claim)
- ✓ #8 Break parallel structure
- ✓ #9 Callbacks (to earlier expert points)
- ✗ #10 Self-correct (don't undermine authority mid-piece)
- ✓ #11 End without wrap-up (let final claim land)
