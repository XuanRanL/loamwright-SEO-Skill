# Spelling: en-CA

Canadian English. **The interesting one** — Canada mixes UK and US spelling depending on word.

## Canadian spelling rules

| Canadian uses UK form | Canadian uses US form |
|---|---|
| colour | aluminum |
| favour | tire (NOT tyre) |
| labour | program (NOT programme even for TV) |
| behaviour | gray AND grey both common |
| centre, metre, litre | curb (NOT kerb) |
| defence | jail (NOT gaol) |
| catalogue | check (verb; cheque for the bank slip noun) |
| traveller | analog (NOT analogue) |
| organize / organise | both acceptable (lean to "ize") |
| realize / realise | both acceptable (lean to "ize") |

The "-ize" vs "-ise" choice in Canada often follows US ("ize") except in journalism (CBC uses both).

## Numbers + units

- Currency: `C$1,234.56` or `CDN$1,234.56` (when distinguishing from US$)
- Distance: kilometres (km), metres (m). Imperial in informal contexts (miles, feet).
- Weight: kilograms (kg) official; pounds informal
- Temperature: °C
- Date format: ISO `2026-05-19` preferred, also `May 19, 2026` (US-style)
- Phone: `+1 (416) 555-1234` (10-digit; same as US format)

## Punctuation

- Mix of US + UK conventions
- Quotes: US-style typically (comma inside)
- Em-dashes: zero tolerance per P13
- Bilingual notes: French content has different rules (see fr.md if doing Quebec content)

## Tone defaults

- Politeness explicit ("please", "thank you" mandatory)
- "Sorry" as filler (Canadianism)
- Direct but not aggressive
- Hockey + nature references resonate

## Bilingual considerations

For Canadian sites targeting Quebec or bilingual markets:
- Some content needs French version (see `references/localization/fr.md`)
- Federal regulations require French equivalents
- Quebec specifically has Bill 96 — strict French language requirements

For purely en-CA content (not bilingual): no French needed.

## Regulatory references
