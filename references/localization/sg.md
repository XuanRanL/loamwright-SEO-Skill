## Language characteristics

**English is the working language**:
- Use standard British English spelling (colour, organise, centre)
- Government + business: very formal
- B2C lifestyle: more casual
- **Singlish**: pidgin English with Malay + Chinese influences (e.g., "lah", "lor", "alamak"). Use only in lifestyle / very casual.

**Mandarin Chinese (zh-SG)**:
- Use **Simplified Chinese** (like mainland China)
- Singapore has its own slight vocabulary variants
- For Chinese-targeting content: use `references/localization/cn.md` as base + adjust SG specifics

## Market characteristics

- 5.6M people; very high purchasing power
- Asia-Pacific business hub
- Tech + finance sectors dominant
- High AI / SaaS adoption
- Multi-ethnic + multilingual

- F1 Singapore GP (night race)
- Strict laws (no chewing gum import, no jaywalking)

## Tone calibration

- Direct + efficient (Asian business style with English directness)
- Formal default for B2B
- Casual + warm for lifestyle/B2C
- "lah" / "lor" Singlish particles: only in true lifestyle content; avoid in business
- Multiracial sensitivity required (don't favor one ethnic group)

## Distinctive features

- **Multilingual mixing**: English with occasional Chinese / Malay phrases natural
- **Localized brand awareness**: "Sheng Siong", "NTUC FairPrice", "Foodpanda" (vs general regional)
- **Hawker references**: cultural shorthand (chicken rice, laksa, kaya toast)
- **Singlish particles**: "lah" (assertion), "lor" (resignation), "leh" (mild emphasis), "alamak" (surprise)

## When to use Singlish

| Content type | Singlish OK? |
|---|---|
| B2B SaaS marketing | ❌ No |
| B2C lifestyle | ⚠️ Sparingly, with context |
| Food / travel | ✓ Adds authenticity |
| Personal blog | ✓ Yes |
| Government / legal | ❌ Never |

Overuse of Singlish = "trying too hard" by foreign brands.
